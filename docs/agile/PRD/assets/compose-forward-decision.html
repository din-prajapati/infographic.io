Buildographic · architecture decision

Extract or Compose Forward

Two routes to an editable design. One is shipped and working; the other is mostly built and idle. The choice is narrower than it first looked.

correction to my earlier advice

I argued compose-forward was needed because OCR could misread a price — a compliance risk. That was wrong. text-block.mapper.ts:13 is explicit: “canonical values are NEVER sourced from model output.” Extraction uses OCR to find where text sits and to match it to a field; the content is re-rendered from your own listing record.

Accuracy is already solved. Removing that plank, compose-forward is a cost and latency optimisation — not a correctness fix. That materially changes the decision, so it belongs at the top rather than in a footnote.

Pros and cons

A · Extract backward

shipped · working

for

  • +
    The AI designs the whole composition. Typography is part of the artwork — weight, spacing and colour respond to the photo behind it.
  • +
    Effectively unlimited layouts. Every generation can be composed differently.
  • +
    Works on any image, including uploads we did not generate.
  • +
    Content is already exact — canonical values, not OCR output.
  • +
    It is done. Verified live: 201 in 27s, cached thereafter.

against

  • −
    $0.09 and 15–90s on first edit per variation.
  • −
    Beta endpoint. Curved or decorative type may not be detected; undetected fields fall back to coarse inferred placement.
  • −
    Two-step UX. Generate, wait, click, wait again.
  • −
    Brand assets stay baked in — no headshot or logo as its own layer.

B · Compose forward

~2/3 built · idle

for

  • +
    Free and instant. No compose call, no $0.09, no 27-second wait.
  • +
    Editable at first render. The second action disappears entirely.
  • +
    Deterministic placement. No detection step means no detection failures.
  • +
    Unlocks brand layers — headshot, logo, licence, QR placed as real elements.
  • +
    Testable and cacheable without a provider in the loop.

against

  • −
    The design may simply look worse. Flat text laid over a photo rarely matches typography the model integrated into the scene. This is the real risk.
  • −
    Three layout templates versus the model's effectively unlimited range — output starts to feel templated.
  • −
    Variations converge. Three results would differ only in background, sharing one text layout.
  • −
    “No text” is a weak instruction. Image models leak text anyway; a leak means doubled copy.
  • −
    The planner is unproven. 49 unit tests, zero production traffic.
  • −
    Does not remove extraction — uploads still need it.

The actual trade

With accuracy off the table, this reduces to one question:

Is $0.09 and a 27-second wait worth more than the AI designing your typography?

Extraction is cached after the first call and only charged when someone actually clicks Edit — so the cost is bounded and lands only on engaged users. Against that, AI-integrated typography is a visible part of why the output looks professional, and we currently hold three layout templates to replace it with.

My recommendation: do not migrate wholesale. The case I originally made for it rested on an accuracy problem that does not exist. What remains is real but modest, and the aesthetic downside is the hardest thing to reverse once users see it.

What I would do instead

Three narrower moves capture most of the value without betting the product's look on three templates:

MoveWhySize
Brand assets as real layers Headshot, logo and licence are ours — baking them in only to extract them back is pure waste, and it never competes with the AI's typography. M
Compose-forward behind a flag, on the photo path only US-AI-051's text-free prompt already exists there. Run it as an A/B on real listings and look at the output before committing. S
Keep extraction as the default It works, it is cached, and it handles uploads — which compose-forward never will. —

The deciding evidence is not in this document — it is a side-by-side of the same listing rendered both ways. That is a day of work and it settles the argument properly.
